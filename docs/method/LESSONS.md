# Lessons

Every mistake below became one line in the job description or in `SKILL.md`. The next machine starts with all of them. Add a row each time you correct Claude.

## From this repo's machines

| What happened | What it looked like | The line it added |
|---|---|---|
| A Ragdoll cat built from a reference picture straight away: no checklist, no measured proportions, no still loop | The owner called it ugly; a head-only redo in code didn't save it and it was dropped | Reference builds go through the method: measure, checklist, grading still, stop after round 2 |
| Near-white armour under the key light on the dark console | Plates blown out to flat white, no shape left | Near-white plates at or below #c0c4be, roughness at least 0.5 |
| The pangolin's tongue fired as its beat began | It pointed at the floor and was finished before the camera arrived | The hero action is aimed at its target from the part's own kinematics, and its event comes after the camera settles |
| The rolled pangolin's tail kept the walking curl | It spiralled inward through the ball | Curls get numbers per segment (10° near the body, 6° at the tip) |
| The scale rings kept their walking spread when rolled | A hollow wheel, not a ball | Rings wrap further when rolled (×1.42) and the tail yaws across the side to close it |
| The tour's clock went negative on frame 0 | The thumbnail carried a pre-roll caption | Frame 0 shows the first beat's caption and callouts |

## From the cutaway batch the method came from

| What happened | What it looked like | The line it added |
|---|---|---|
| A checklist said "thin bristles" | Quills twice the length they should be | Bristles and spikes are 0.15 to 0.25 of the limb's thickness |
| A rule kept the whole octopus in frame | The camera pulled back to a flat view from above, arms tucked in: a toy | Only the part doing the work has to stay in view |
| A moth, a hydra and a fly graded themselves dense enough | Clean white diagrams; the moth was thrown out after 9 rounds | Density gets measured: dark share at least 0.8 of the picture's |
| A piranha passed on density in round 2 | A crocodile: jaws at 0.52 of the body length against 0.37 in the picture | Proportions get measured off the picture before the first line of code |
| A flying octopus and a scorpion-squid | Wings and tentacles fighting for the same half second | One main set of limbs per creature |
| Glow dots sprinkled over shells to add accents | Polka dots | The accent as lines, never isolated dots |
| A film ported from another body's choreography with an extra rule | Far away, flat, a toy | Port the closest approved film 1:1 and change the body only |
| A scarab with no close body | 4 rounds, about 450,000 tokens, still busy and cheap | A subject with no close body gets flagged before it starts |
| Every beat filmed from one side | The camera sat between 20° and 50°, at one height and one distance | Every beat gets its own side, height and distance, aimed at its part |
| Short versions cut from the long film's busiest moments | Jumps from fragment to fragment | A short version is its own script with its own opening and exit |
| 4 builds at once, reviewed after round 6 | One moth alone ate about 430,000 tokens before it was dropped | 2 builds at a time, reviewed after round 2 |
